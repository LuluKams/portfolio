Housing Inequality & Eviction Drivers in the U.S.
Business Problem
Housing instability and eviction risk vary dramatically across the U.S., but the drivers were unclear and the data was large, messy, and drawn from disconnected sources.
Objective
Transform ambiguous, large-scale, multi-source data into clear recommendations for policy and community stakeholders.
Approach
Conducted an end-to-end analysis across 3,000+ U.S. counties and 6 data sources, handling data ambiguity at scale through rigorous cleaning, normalization, and feature selection.
Stakeholders
Policy and community stakeholders focused on housing stability.
Analysis
Identified a 2x+ eviction rate disparity between the lowest and highest income quintiles, with distinct geographic patterns.
Recommendations
Synthesized findings into clear, policy-relevant recommendations pinpointing where structural drivers of housing instability were most severe.
Business Impact
Demonstrated the ability to transform ambiguous, large-scale data into insights decision-makers could act on directly.
